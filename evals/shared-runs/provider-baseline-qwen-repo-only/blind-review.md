### The Gradient Funeral

- So. Roastr. The tagline is 'Research-led parody videos for repos and products.' Beautiful. Aspirational. And the production video collection—line four of LandingPage.tsx, right there in plain sight—is `const approvedVideos: ReviewVideo[] = []`. Empty. You've built a cinema, bought the projector, hired the popcorn guy, and the reel is a blank leader. The audience is an empty array. The popcorn guy is `aria-hidden="true"`.
- And the demos—the ones you'd point to and say 'see, the videos!'—shared.js opens with a comment that reads like a small, polite apology: 'There are no real roast videos yet. Every video in this prototype is a CSS placeholder: a two-stop gradient poster with a drifting sheen so the eye reads it as motion.' A drifting sheen. You've given your CI pipeline a eulogy—'A funeral for the build that died 40 times this week'—but the funeral is a radial-gradient from #ff4d3d to #7a1020 with a white highlight that slides across it. The dead build is not in the coffin. The dead build IS the coffin. It's the gradient. It's the sheen. It drifted away.
- Now, I'll grant you this: you're cost-conscious. The image model is Flux Schnell, 512 by 512, one inference step, one image, JPEG. One. Step. That's not an image. That's a smudge. That's the visual equivalent of saying 'I'll be right back' to a waiter in a restaurant that closes in four minutes. The README even justifies it: 'to minimize cost.' You minimized cost the way a hamster minimizes the wheel. You are the wheel. You are the hamster. The wheel is $0.003 per megapixel and you are running it once.
- And the repository description—what a stranger sees before they even clone—is: 'No repository description.' Not 'coming soon.' Not 'a work in progress.' No. Description. The roast platform that reads your README back to you 'under oath' is the one whose own README metadata is a shrug. The README Oath template would open with the attorney standing at the podium, looking at the camera, and saying: '…nothing.' Just… nothing. A long pause. Then: 'Next case.'
- And look, in the wild, people have reacted to this repo by… well, they've reacted by not reacting. There's no issue thread. No 'this is cool!' star. The closest public signal is the proxy.ts file, where a developer rewrote the root route to the prototype landing page because, and I quote, 'that file is owned by other work in this app.' The landing page isn't the product. The landing page is a hostage. It was taken by the prototype and left with a note that says 'we'll get back to you.' And the prototype's videos are a gradient. So the hostage is a gradient. The gradient is the hostage.
- Here's the thing I keep coming back to, and I will say it once and then I will not say it again: you built a machine that watches a repo, researches it, and performs a parody monologue about its flaws. And the machine's own flaw is that it has no monologue. The machine's own flaw is that the monologue is a CSS file. The machine's own flaw is that the audience is `[]`. If Roastr roasts itself, the script is: 'Your repo has notes for you.' And the notes are a two-color gradient. And the notes are drifting. And there is no one in the room to read them. Just the sheen. Just the sheen, going back and forth, back and forth, like a lighthouse with no ship and no coast and no lighthouse keeper. Just the sheen. Drifting. In an empty array.
